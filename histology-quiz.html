Part I • MCQ

Choose the single best answer

Question 1

Which layer of the skin is composed of keratinized stratified squamous epithelium?

Answer: Epidermis. The epidermis is keratinized stratified squamous epithelium.
Question 2

The histologic terms “thick skin” and “thin skin” primarily refer to differences in:

Answer: Epidermal thickness. They refer to epidermal thickness, especially the stratum corneum.
Question 3

Which epidermal layer is normally well seen only in thick skin?

Answer: Lucidum. Stratum lucidum is characteristic of thick skin.
Question 4

The mitotically active stem/progenitor compartment of the epidermis is concentrated in the:

Answer: Stratum basale. Basal cells generate new keratinocytes.
Question 5

The “spines” of stratum spinosum are best explained by:

Answer: Desmosomal attachments between keratinocytes. Desmosomes persist as cells shrink during preparation, producing intercellular bridges.
Question 6

Keratohyalin granules are most characteristic of the:

Answer: Granulosum. The stratum granulosum contains conspicuous keratohyalin granules.
Question 7

Filaggrin primarily helps:

Answer: Aggregate keratin filaments. Filaggrin promotes aggregation of keratin filaments.
Question 8

Lamellar bodies are especially important for formation of the:

Answer: Epidermal water barrier. Their lipid-rich contents are released at the granulosum-corneum interface.
Question 9

The most superficial epidermal cells are typically:

Answer: Anucleate squames filled with keratin. Corneocytes are flattened, anucleate, and keratin-filled.
Question 10

Basal keratinocytes are attached to the basal lamina mainly by:

Answer: Hemidesmosomes. Hemidesmosomes anchor basal cells to basal lamina.
Question 11

The predominant epidermal cell type is the:

Answer: Keratinocyte. Keratinocytes account for about 85% of epidermal cells.
Question 12

Melanocytes are derived embryologically from:

Answer: Neural crest. Melanocyte precursors migrate from the neural crest.
Question 13

Melanin synthesis begins with tyrosinase-mediated conversion of tyrosine to:

Answer: DOPA. Tyrosinase oxidizes tyrosine to DOPA.
Question 14

Transfer of melanosomes from melanocyte processes to keratinocytes is called:

Answer: Cytocrine secretion/pigment donation. Keratinocytes phagocytose tips of melanocyte processes with melanosomes.
Question 15

A melanocyte is associated with approximately how many keratinocytes in the epidermal–melanin unit?

Answer: 36. The chapter gives ~1 melanocyte to ~36 keratinocytes.
Question 16

Which epidermal cell is an antigen-presenting cell?

Answer: Langerhans cell. Langerhans cells provide immunosurveillance and antigen presentation.
Question 17

Which cell is associated with a sensory nerve ending and mechanoreception?

Answer: Merkel cell. Merkel cells participate in cutaneous mechanosensation.
Question 18

The papillary dermis consists primarily of:

Answer: Loose CT with thin collagen fibers. Papillary dermis is loose CT, rich in small vessels and sensory endings.
Question 19

The reticular dermis is best described as:

Answer: Dense irregular CT with thick type I collagen bundles. Reticular dermis is dense irregular CT and is thicker/less cellular.
Question 20

A small encapsulated receptor in a dermal papilla immediately under the epidermis is most likely a:

Answer: Meissner corpuscle. Meissner corpuscles are superficial touch receptors in dermal papillae.
Question 21

A large onion-like lamellated structure in the hypodermis is a:

Answer: Pacinian corpuscle. Pacinian corpuscles detect deep pressure and vibration.
Question 22

Ruffini corpuscles are associated primarily with:

Answer: Skin stretch and torque. Ruffini endings respond to mechanical displacement/stretch.
Question 23

The hair matrix is located in the:

Answer: Hair bulb. Matrix cells around the dermal papilla in the bulb generate hair.
Question 24

Which hair shaft layer makes up most of hair mass?

Answer: Cortex. The cortex accounts for about 80% of hair mass.
Question 25

The follicular bulge is important because it contains:

Answer: Epidermal stem cells. The bulge is a stem-cell reservoir.
Question 26

Sebaceous glands use which mode of secretion?

Answer: Holocrine. Whole secretory cells degenerate and become part of sebum.
Question 27

Sebaceous glands usually empty into the:

Answer: Hair follicle infundibulum. Sebum enters the pilosebaceous canal/hair follicle.
Question 28

Which gland is independent of hair follicles and has a major thermoregulatory role?

Answer: Eccrine sweat. Eccrine glands open to the surface and cool by evaporation.
Question 29

Compared with eccrine glands, apocrine glands characteristically have:

Answer: Much wider lumina. A wide lumen is a classic histologic clue for apocrine glands.
Question 30

Despite their name, human apocrine sweat glands release their secretory product mainly by:

Answer: Merocrine exocytosis. The chapter states TEM shows merocrine secretion.
Question 31

The duct of an eccrine gland is typically lined by:

Answer: Stratified cuboidal epithelium. The eccrine duct is two-cell-layer stratified cuboidal epithelium.
Question 32

Which structure is made of hard keratin?

Answer: Nail plate. Nails contain hard keratin.
Question 33

Which is most consistent with thick skin?

Answer: No hair follicles + many eccrine glands. Thick skin is hairless, has thick corneum, prominent ridges, and many eccrine glands.
Question 34

Skin incisions made parallel to Langer’s lines tend to:

Answer: Heal with less scarring. Langer's lines reflect collagen tension lines; parallel incisions generally scar less.
Question 35

A full-thickness third-degree burn may fail to reepithelialize from the wound bed because:

Answer: All local epithelial appendage/stem-cell reservoirs may be destroyed. Hair follicles and other adnexa can supply epithelial cells; complete destruction removes these local sources.
