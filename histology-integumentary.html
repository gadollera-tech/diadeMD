01 • OVERVIEW

Start with the architecture of skin.

The integumentary system consists of skin and its epidermal appendages. Skin itself has two main layers: epidermis and dermis. The hypodermis lies deep to the skin and contains variable adipose tissue.

E

Epidermis

Keratinized stratified squamous epithelium. Continuously renewed and derived from ectoderm.

D

Dermis

Connective-tissue support layer derived from mesoderm. Papillary + reticular divisions.

H

Hypodermis

Subcutaneous fascia with variable adipose tissue arranged in lobules separated by connective-tissue septa.

Very high yield: “Thick skin” and “thin skin” refer to the epidermis, not the total thickness of skin. Thick skin is found on palms and soles, is hairless, has a much thicker stratum corneum, and shows stratum lucidum best.

02 • EPIDERMIS

Deep → superficial: Basale, Spinosum, Granulosum, Lucidum, Corneum.

LayerHistologic clueMain event
Stratum basaleSingle layer of small cuboidal/low columnar cells on basal laminaMitotic renewal; hemidesmosomes to basal lamina; desmosomes between neighboring cells
Stratum spinosumSeveral layers of “prickle” cells with intercellular bridgesKeratin synthesis continues; tonofilaments bundle into tonofibrils; desmosomes produce the spiny appearance
Stratum granulosumFlattened cells with intensely basophilic keratohyalin granulesFilaggrin-related keratin aggregation; lamellar bodies contribute to the water barrier
Stratum lucidumPale/refractile bandAdvanced keratinization; best seen in thick skin
Stratum corneumFlattened anucleate squamesKeratin-filled terminal cells; lipid barrier; continuous desquamation
Turnover numbers from the chapter: roughly 1–2 days for basal mitotic divisions, about 31 days through the keratinocyte compartment, plus about 14 days in the stratum corneum → total approximately 47 days. In psoriasis, turnover can shorten to about 8–10 days.

Epidermal water barrier

Terminally differentiating keratinocytes create both a protein-rich cell envelope and an external lipid envelope. Lamellar bodies discharge lipid-rich material between the stratum granulosum and stratum corneum. Loss of this barrier over large areas can cause dangerous fluid loss.

03 • EPIDERMAL CELLS

Four cells. Four jobs. Do not mix them up.

CellWhere / clueFunctionMust remember
KeratinocytePredominant cell in all epidermal layersKeratin production + water-barrier formation~85% of epidermal cells
MelanocyteCell body in basale; dendritic processes between keratinocytesMelanin production and UV protectionNeural crest derived; pigment donation to keratinocytes; ~1 melanocyte associates with ~36 keratinocytes
Langerhans cellDendritic APC, especially in spinosum; difficult to identify with routine H&EAntigen processing and immune surveillanceBone-marrow derived; Birbeck granules on TEM
Merkel cellBasal layer, closely associated with a sensory nerve endingCutaneous mechanoreceptionMerkel cell + disc receptor = Merkel corpuscle
Melanin pathway: tyrosine --tyrosinase→ DOPA → melanin. Melanosomes mature and move along melanocyte dendrites. Keratinocytes phagocytose melanocyte-process tips during pigment donation, and melanin accumulates above keratinocyte nuclei as UV-protective “umbrellas.”

Skin color clues

The chapter emphasizes that melanocyte number is essentially similar across races; pigmentation differences depend strongly on melanin production, melanosome distribution, and degradation. It distinguishes eumelanin (brown-black) from pheomelanin (reddish-yellow), and notes that albinism may involve absent tyrosinase activity despite formation of premelanosomes.

04 • DERMIS

Papillary is superficial and loose. Reticular is deep and dense.

FeaturePapillary layerReticular layer
CT typeLoose connective tissueDense irregular connective tissue
CollagenThin fibers, mainly types I and IIIThick irregular bundles, mostly type I
CellularityMore cellularLess cellular
Key contentsCapillaries, lymphatics, sensory endings in dermal papillaeLarger vessels, appendages, coarse elastic fibers, deeper receptors
Interface: dermal papillae project upward while epidermal/rete ridges project downward. This enlarges the junction and strengthens attachment. In palmar and plantar skin, ridge patterns form the basis of dermatoglyphics.

Langer’s lines reflect organized lines of tension formed by collagen and elastic fibers in the reticular dermis. Incisions parallel to these lines generally heal with less scarring.

05 • SENSORY RECEPTORS

For histology, location is half the diagnosis.

F

Free nerve endings

Most numerous. Fine touch, heat, cold, and pain. Unencapsulated; may extend into the epidermis and surround hair follicles.

M

Meissner corpuscle

Light touch. Small elongated receptor within dermal papillae immediately beneath epidermis, especially hairless skin.

P

Pacinian corpuscle

Deep pressure and vibration. Large ovoid “onion-like” concentric lamellae in deep dermis/hypodermis.

R

Ruffini corpuscle

Responds to skin stretch and torque through displacement of collagen fibers.

K

Merkel corpuscle

Merkel cell plus an afferent disc receptor; sensitive mechanoreceptor in the basal epidermis.

Microscope shortcut: Meissner = superficial papilla. Pacinian = deep onion.
06 • HAIR

Bulb makes the hair; bulge stores the stem cells.

A hair follicle is an epidermal invagination. The expanded inferior end forms the bulb, which is invaginated by a vascular connective-tissue dermal papilla. Rapidly dividing matrix cells around the papilla differentiate into the hair shaft and internal root sheath.

StructureHigh-yield point
Hair shaftMedulla + cortex + cuticle; cortex contributes about 80% of hair mass
Internal root sheathHenle layer + Huxley layer + internal root sheath cuticle
External root sheathContinuous with epidermis
Follicular bulgeStem-cell reservoir near arrector pili insertion
Hair bulb / matrixRapidly proliferating cells surrounding the dermal papilla produce hair
Arrector piliSmooth muscle; contraction produces hair erection and can help discharge sebum
Hair cycle: anagen = growth, catagen = brief regression, telogen = resting phase. The chapter notes that more than 80% of normal scalp hairs are in anagen.
07 • GLANDS

Sebaceous, eccrine, apocrine: compare them side by side.

Sebaceous gland

Sebaceous glands develop as outgrowths of the external root sheath and usually drain into hair follicles. The central cells become filled with lipid and die; the entire cell contributes to sebum, making this a holocrine secretion.

Slide clue: pale/foamy lobules next to a hair follicle. Peripheral basal cells are smaller and darker; central cells are lipid-filled and progressively degenerating.

08 • NAILS

Hard keratin made at the matrix.

M

Nail matrix/root

Proliferating cells differentiate to produce hard nail keratin. The matrix contains stem cells plus several epidermal cell types.

P

Nail plate

Closely packed anucleate corneocytes containing hard keratin. It grows distally over the nail bed.

B

Nail bed

Epithelium continuous with stratum basale/spinosum plus underlying dermis.

TermMeaning
LunulaWhite crescent near the root produced by thick, opaque, partially keratinized matrix cells
EponychiumCuticle / skin fold covering the root of the nail
HyponychiumThickened epidermal layer securing the free nail edge at the fingertip
09 • CLINICAL CORRELATIONS

Keep the histology tied to what can go wrong.

BCC

Basal cell carcinoma

Resembles cells of the stratum basale; described as slow growing and usually nonmetastatic in the chapter.

SCC

Squamous cell carcinoma

Atypical keratinocytes can involve all epidermal levels; basement-membrane disruption allows invasion and spread.

M

Malignant melanoma

Melanocyte-derived malignancy. The chapter reviews radial and vertical growth and the ABCD warning pattern.

P

Psoriasis

Hyperproliferative epidermal disorder with markedly shortened turnover time and thick scaly plaques.

A

Acne / sebum

The chapter links sebum retention in the hair-follicle isthmus and bacterial breakdown of triglycerides to acne lesions.

CF

Sweat as a disease clue

Elevated sweat sodium and chloride are noted as indicators of cystic fibrosis; urea frost is discussed in pronounced uremia.

Skin repair

Primary union occurs in clean wounds whose edges are approximated, such as sutured surgical incisions. Secondary union occurs in wounds with separated edges and greater tissue loss, requiring more granulation tissue. Basal keratinocytes proliferate and migrate beneath the scab; epithelial stem-cell reservoirs in hair follicles can contribute to resurfacing after epidermal loss.

Burn clue: massive destruction of all epithelial structures, as in a full-thickness third-degree burn, removes local epithelial reservoirs and may require grafting for effective reepithelialization.
